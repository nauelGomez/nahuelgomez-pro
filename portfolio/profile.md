## Systems in production

Three systems, all running right now.

### 01 · mëtalix

**Own product · Live**

Multi-tenant SaaS for scrapyard management, built end to end.

- **Problem.** Scrapyards run appointments, weigh-ins and payments on
  spreadsheets or one-off systems built by hand for each business. No way to
  scale, no way to onboard a new client quickly.
- **What I did.** The whole product, backend and frontend, with data isolation
  verified across three layers of code and tests, plus an admin panel for
  signup, plans, roles and subscription billing.
- **Outcome.** In production, with active customers paying today. Adding a
  business means registering it, not deploying another instance.

**Built with:** TypeScript · Express 5 · Prisma · PostgreSQL · React 19

Includes a logistics calendar with scheduling, routes and tracking in one view,
and a multi-tenant panel where every business has its own plan and users on the
same codebase.

### 02 · Sastre

**Own platform · 2 live stores**

The multi-store ecommerce platform running two real businesses today.

- **Problem.** Every new store meant another server, another database and
  another thing to maintain in parallel. It did not scale.
- **What I did.** A single API with its admin panel, designed so that opening a
  store means registering it, not standing up infrastructure. Stock per variant
  and bulk catalogue upload from Excel.
- **Outcome.** Two live stores on the same codebase: Suburbia, on its own domain
  and selling, and Play Zone Mar del Plata.

**Built with:** Node · Express · Prisma · PostgreSQL · Railway

**Live stores:**
- Suburbia — https://suburbiauy.com (own domain, selling)
- Play Zone — Mar del Plata

Stock per variant (size and colour with their own inventory) and CSV reports
with templates and one-click export.

### 03 · Truck and Drinks & Barbac

**Contract work · In use**

Two custom management systems, built around how each business actually operates.

- **Problem.** Businesses running on spreadsheets and generic software that does
  not reflect how they actually work: stock, events and quotes in one case,
  internal management in the other.
- **What I did.** I mapped each business's real process and modelled the system
  on it, not the other way round. Truck and Drinks runs its own calculation
  engines for stock, events and quotes.
- **Outcome.** Both in active use by their clients, with maintenance and new
  features on request.

**Built with:** PHP / Symfony · Symfony 8 · Angular · MongoDB · PostgreSQL ·
AWS with Docker · Railway

Includes real-time deposits and inventory, and logistics recommendations using
advanced operations research: optimal routes, loads and timing per event.
